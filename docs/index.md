# Welcome to NoSE!

## Project overview

**NoSE: North Sea-Atlantic Exchange** is an ongoing [NWO](https://www.nwo.nl/)-funded scientific research project.

The overarching aim of NoSE is to constrain the past, present and future exchange of carbon and other essential
nutrients between the North Sea and the Atlantic Ocean.  To achieve this, NoSE is addressing the following key research questions:

  1. How much carbon and nutrients are exported from the North Sea and what is the balance between burial in North
Sea sediments and export to the Atlantic Ocean?
  2. Which physical, biological and chemical processes control sediment transport and recycling and burial of carbon and
nutrients in the North Sea seafloor?
  3. What are the natural and anthropogenic drivers of temporal variability in cycling and transport of carbon and
nutrients in the North Sea, on timescales from weeks to millennia?
  4. How will carbon and nutrient export from shelf seas like the North Sea, and thus their wider biogeochemical
influence, respond to local and global environmental change?

Together with the NoSE PIs and project partners, a team of six PhD students and three postdocs are exploring these questions using results from research expeditions and modelling.

## NoSE consortium

The NoSE principal investigators are based at the [NIOZ Royal Netherlands Institute for Sea Research](https://www.nioz.nl/), [Delft University of Technology](https://www.tudelft.nl/) (DUT), [Utrecht University](https://www.uu.nl) (UU) and the [University of Groningen](https://www.rug.nl) (RUG).  Our project partners include researchers from [Deltares](https://www.deltares.nl/) and the [Koninklijk Nederlands Meteorologisch Instituut](https://www.knmi.nl/) (KNMI) in the Netherlands as well as the [University of Bergen](https://www4.uib.no/) (Norway), [Helmholtz-Zentrum Hereon](https://www.hereon.de/) (Germany), [Plymouth Marine Laboratory](https://pml.ac.uk) (UK) and the [University of East Anglia](https://www.uea.ac.uk) (UK) internationally.
